PBOC Expands e-CNY Cross-Border Pilot to 28 Cities

Caixin | March 11, 2026 09:42 Beijing | TrexID: CX-2026-0311

The People's Bank of China on March 11 announced that the digital yuan (e-CNY) cross-border settlement pilot will expand from 14 cities to 28 major cities nationwide, with bilateral digital currency bridge agreements reached with six ASEAN central banks. This marks the largest expansion since the e-CNY pilot began in 2024.

Mu Changchun, director of the PBOC's Digital Currency Research Institute, said at a press conference that the new phase will focus on three areas: full coverage of cross-border trade settlement scenarios, deep integration with commercial banks' existing systems, and exploration of smart-contract-based automated clearing.

"We have completed technical integration tests with MAS, the Bank of Thailand, and Bank Negara Malaysia," Mu said. "Daily processing capacity has reached 150,000 cross-border transactions, with single settlement time under three seconds."

Core technical upgrades include a new distributed ledger architecture supporting over 10,000 TPS—about eight times the previous generation—and a zero-knowledge privacy layer so counterparty data stays off third-party systems while meeting regulatory requirements.

Analysts expect the move to significantly increase yuan usage in ASEAN. UBS Asia Pacific chief economist Tao Wang said that if e-CNY cross-border settlement rolls out across the region, the yuan's share of regional trade settlement could rise from about 12% now to 25–30% by 2028.

The central bank also released the "e-CNY Open Banking Protocol v2.0" API standard for financial institutions, allowing third-party payment and fintech firms to plug into the e-CNY clearing network via standardized interfaces—seen as a major step toward an open e-CNY ecosystem.

Some industry figures struck a cautious note. A fintech head at a large state-owned bank told Caixin that cross-border settlement involves compliance across jurisdictions, and that "technical connectivity is only the first step; the real challenge is coordination and mutual recognition of regulatory frameworks."
